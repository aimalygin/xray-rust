# XHTTP H3 downloads: a receive budget for readers that fall behind

Measured **2026-10-31 11:49–14:00 America/Chicago**: Linux in Docker Desktop
29.6.1's VM on the Apple M6 with the setup of the
[download-memory report](../2026-10-31-xhttp-h3-download-memory/README.md),
and macOS on the same M6 (Mac18,5, macOS 27.0) with the migration collector.
The server is Xray-core v26.10.21 throughout.

On Linux the client drains 32 downloads slower than Xray-core sends them, so
every download's 2 MiB QUIC stream window stays full: 57 MiB of the 96 MiB
peak. Concurrent XHTTP H3 downloads now share a receive budget of 16 MiB, or
4 MiB with the mobile and low-memory profiles. It holds a download to its
share only once that download's reader has fallen behind its stream. In
release builds, 32 downloads peak at **50.0 instead of 96.7 MiB RSS** and 32
duplex transfers at **63.6 instead of 110.6 MiB**. A transfer four times as
long peaks at 50.5 instead of 97.7 MiB, and with the mobile profile 32
downloads peak at 43.5 instead of 96.3 MiB. Throughput rose 0.8–6.6% and CPU
per byte fell 0.7–5.5%.

One- and eight-flow cases on Linux stay within the run-to-run spread, as do
the macOS collector's twelve `xhttp-h3-*` cases and eight downloads over 54
and 114 ms round trips. A plain cap, the first version, saved a little more
memory but held eight downloads over a 54 ms round trip to 1.4 instead of
3.5 Gbit/s.

## Policy

A connection counts as downloading once it has read 64 KiB, and samples its
share again every 64 KiB. The budget is split between the connections that
sampled during the current or the previous second. This is the send budget's
`UploadActivity`, renamed `TransferActivity` and now shared by both
directions.

A connection's share is the budget divided by that count, never below
128 KiB. The budget is never less than the connection's largest auto-tuned
window, so a lone download still reaches it, and eight downloads keep the
2 MiB they start with. The 2 MiB stream and 3 MiB connection transport
parameters are unchanged, as in Xray's client. A smaller share applies once
the reader has passed the handshake credit, because credit already granted
is never withdrawn.

The share holds a connection only while its reader is behind. The response
task notes when a read finds the stream empty, which means nothing waits in
memory. A connection is held once its reader has gone four samples (256 KiB)
and two round trips, and at least 10 ms, without catching up. Even then its
window is never smaller than what the reader took in those round trips. When
the reader catches up again, its allowance doubles at every sample, back
toward the auto-tuned window.

Vendored quinn-proto's `set_stream_receive_window` could only grow the
window. It now also lowers it without withdrawing credit:

- `Recv::max_stream_data` never offers less than the stream's last sent
  limit, so a lost update is resent whole.
- A new or reused stream starts from the handshake window, which is the
  credit its peer actually holds.

`XRAY-PATCH.diff` was regenerated by a procedure that first reproduced the
committed diff byte for byte.

## The way there

Each version, paired with the `before` runs of its own campaign. Cells are
the median paired change, with the number of pairs.

| Version | 32 downloads, peak RSS | 8 downloads, about 54 ms | 8 downloads, 112–116 ms |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| cap: every downloading connection held to its share | 96.0 → 41.0 MiB | −59.5% (3) | −35.6% (3) |
| behind: held after four samples without catching up | 96.0 → 48.3 MiB | −44.6% (3) | −18.2% (3) |
| timed: also two round trips | 96.5 → 50.1 MiB | −10.2% (4), −13.0% (8), −0.9% (8) | +4.2% (4), +2.8% (6) |
| floor: never below two round trips' reads | 96.7 → 50.0 MiB | −3.6% (8) | −0.9% (6) |

**cap** limits all concurrent downloads together to the budget per round
trip: 16 MiB per 54 ms is 2.5 Gbit/s, and per 113 ms 1.19 Gbit/s. With the
constrained 4 MiB budget, that is 297 Mbit/s at 113 ms.

**behind** spared readers that keep up, but each endpoint hands over up to
four datagrams of 64 coalesced segments at once, about 370 KB. That is more
than four samples, so a reader that keeps up still counted as behind within
each burst.

**timed** fixed the bursts, but in the second campaign's slower runs the
client was busy and its readers did fall behind for a while. Their 2 MiB
share was below the roughly 2.9 MB each needed per round trip, and 13% of
throughput went with it. The same comparison an hour later measured −0.9%.

**floor** keeps a held reader's window at what it actually takes, and its
delayed-path changes stay inside the spread of the `before` runs themselves.
Before ranged from 2.70 to 3.99 Gbit/s at 54 ms; floor ranged from 2.62 to
3.86.

## Where the peak sits

`run-mem.sh` sampled RSS every 100 ms in rotated 32-download runs, ten of
before's:

- In seven of before's runs, RSS sat at 90–94 MiB for most of the transfer:
  the windows were full throughout.
- In the other three, the client kept up at 33–37 MiB, and two of those
  spiked to 93 MiB near their end.
- floor sat at 45–48 MiB in all four of its runs, cap at 39–40 and timed at
  35–51.

What remains is mostly what the download-memory report attributed outside
the windows: about 30 MiB of executable, connection, TLS and endpoint
state, and glibc's free heap. The rest is at most a 512 KiB share in each of
32 windows, more for readers that briefly keep up.

## Tests

- **quinn-proto unit test.**
  `a_resized_stream_window_is_offered_from_the_next_read` covers a grown
  window, a smaller one with a resent update and the reader catching up, and
  a stream that opens under the smaller window. Reverting any of the three
  quinn-proto changes fails it, and the library's 299 tests pass.
- **xray-transport unit tests.** `receive_window_tests` covers the share, the
  constrained budget and a lone download, and holding a reader that falls
  behind and releasing it once it catches up. They also check that a 100 ms
  path's bursts do not count as falling behind, and that a held reader keeps
  what it took per round trip. Each of those two fails when its rule is
  removed.
- **End-to-end tests.** `slow_reader_downloads_grant_only_their_share_of_the_receive_budget`
  runs sixteen downloads whose readers pause 25 ms after each 64 KiB. With
  the final code their servers got 15.7–15.8 MiB ahead of the readers in
  all; the test allows at most 18 MiB. With the share removed they got
  29.7 MiB ahead, and the test fails.
  `downloads_whose_readers_keep_up_keep_their_windows` runs sixteen readers
  that keep up with paced servers. Each server stays about 2 MiB ahead,
  31.5 MiB in all, where the plain cap held them to 14.6 MiB and fails the
  test.
  The two share a lock with the auto-tuning test, because every download in
  one process counts toward the same budget.
- **Checks.** `cargo fmt --all --check`, `cargo clippy -p xray-transport
  --all-targets --all-features --locked -- -D warnings` and all
  `xray-transport` tests pass. `RUSTDOCFLAGS=-D warnings cargo doc` still
  fails on a private `crate::stream::h2_bdp` link in `grpc/h2client.rs` that
  predates this change.

## Not covered

- No Android or iOS device. The constrained budget was measured only through
  `XRAY_TUN_PROFILE=mobile` in the Linux VM.
- Loopback with GSO and GRO, and Xray-core's quic-go as the only server. The
  delayed path has no rate limit and no loss.
- The delayed path's own spread is ±10–40% per pair, so it resolves cap's
  and behind's costs but no change smaller than about 10%.
- The budget is soft. A reader that stops reading stops counting after one
  to two seconds and keeps the window it had. A burst of new downloads can
  still fill each 2 MiB handshake window before the first sample.
